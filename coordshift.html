How to use coordshift

  1. Pick your Source CRS

    In the left panel, search by name or EPSG code and select the coordinate reference system your CSV is currently in — e.g. WGS 84, Indiana East, or 6326.

  2. Load your CSV file

    Drag and drop a .csv file anywhere on the page, or click Open CSV in the bottom bar to browse. Your data never leaves the browser.

  3. Confirm X and Y columns

    coordshift auto-detects common column names (lon, lat, x, easting, etc.). If it picks the wrong ones, use the X / Y dropdowns in the bottom bar to correct them.

  4. Verify your points on the map

    Blue dots appear for the first 25 rows. If they land in the right place, your Source CRS is correct. If not, try a different CRS or swap X and Y.

  5. Pick your Target CRS

    Choose the CRS you want to convert to. The list is automatically filtered to options relevant to your data's location. Red dots appear on the map — red dots that overlap the blue dots mean the CRS is correct. Click any dot to inspect the coordinate values.

  6. Convert and Download

    Click Convert to process all rows. The map switches to showing original (blue) vs. converted (red) points side by side. Click ⬇ Download to save the result as a CSV with new columns appended.

Tip: For high-accuracy survey work (NAD83(2011) ↔ ITRF2014), coordshift applies a built-in 14-parameter Helmert transform — no grids or internet needed. For the full Python CLI, run pip install coordshift.
